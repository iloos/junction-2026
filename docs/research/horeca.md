# HoReCa purchasing: how kitchens really order

_Research, Fri 13 Nov 2026. For the Valio Aimo kitchen agent ([CONCEPT](../concept/CONCEPT.md)). Web sources linked inline. **(?)** marks a claim that is uncertain, vendor-sourced or our own estimate._

## 1. Roles and the ordering loop
- **Who orders.** In a restaurant, the head chef or sous chef orders, often late at night or before service. In a hotel the executive chef owns food purchasing, banquets feed in through the F&B / events team, and chains often have central purchasing contracts and a product list that limit what a site may buy **(?, common practice, not sourced)**. Valio Aimo told us to assume the user has full authority.
- **The loop is count, compare to par, order the gap.** A par level is the stock that carries you to the next delivery plus a safety buffer of roughly 20–30% of usage ([Unleashed](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/blog/par-levels-in-inventory-management-with-formula-examples/), [HotSchedules](https://help.hotschedules.com/hc/en-us/articles/360055939952-Inventory-Restaurants-Par-Level-Ordering)). You order *up to* par, not the par amount. Most kitchens count weekly, and busy items daily. Ordering usually climbs a ladder: manual, then order templates, then standing orders, then fill-to-par, then forecast-driven ordering ([Lightspeed](https://lightspeedhq.com/blog/par-inventory-sheet)). The agent is the top step of that ladder.
- **Valio Aimo's channel.** Delivery wholesale ships about 25,000 products from Espoo and Kuopio, usually **within 24 h on agreed delivery days**. The webshop already has order templates, order history, several carts for different delivery dates, planning weeks ahead, and editing a confirmed order ([delivery wholesale](https://www.valioaimo.fi/en/become-a-customer/delivery-wholesale/)). The My Aimo portal adds purchase reports, the **carbon footprint of purchases**, "My Recipes" with margins and add-to-cart, and an assistant called **A.I.mo** that looks up past purchases ([tools](https://www.valioaimo.fi/en/company/why-choose-valio-aimo-as-a-partner/innovative-tools-for-everyday-work/)). Cut-off times are not published, but the mock API's availability response returns the order closing time per line, so cut-offs are real data for us.

## 2. Hotel F&B and events
- **BEO (banquet event order).** This is the signed operating document for one event: date, room, setup, headcount, menu, timings, service and billing. It is drafted 10–30 days ahead ([Roommaster](https://www.roommaster.com/blog/what-is-a-beo), [Amadeus](https://amadeus-hospitality.com/insight/beo-template/)). The client gives a **guaranteed count 48–72 h before** the event, and that count is the minimum billed ([GSA tips](https://www.geosociety.org/GSA/GSA/Section_Manual/Budgets/fbTips.aspx)).
- **Overset.** Hotels typically lay tables for **~3% above the guarantee** (3–5% across the industry) but cook to the guarantee ([Boston Park Plaza](https://issuu.com/bostonparkplaza/docs/banquet_menu_final_060223/s/25703477), [Fairmont](https://issuu.com/fairmontpalliser/docs/catering_menu_2023/s/20800416)). Kitchens tend to over-prepare by 10–15% over the guarantee, which costs them margin ([Restaurant Business](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/rein-catering-operation)).
- **How events deviate from the regular order.** An event is a one-off layer on top of the base order. It has its own menu, a headcount that firms up over time (estimate, then guarantee, then actuals), new SKUs that are not on the par list, and the ordering must start early enough for the cut-off before the delivery day ahead of prep.

## 3. Seasons and anomalies
- **Pikkujoulut (Nov–mid-Dec)** is the most important season of the year for Finnish restaurants and tourism. Expectations are mixed: in Nov 2025 only about a quarter of businesses expected sales to grow, and 20% of food restaurants ([Yrittäjät/MaRa](https://www.yrittajat.fi/uutiset/pikkujoulukausi-pettymys-ravintoloille-iskee-eniten-pieniin-yrityksiin/)). Hotel Christmas and ready-made Christmas Eve tables are a growing trend ([Verkkouutiset](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/jouluaaton-valmis-poyta-ja-hotellijoulu-ovat-nouseva-trendi/)). Other peaks are summer terrace season and the weekday lunch rhythm, where Monday–Friday volumes are steady.
- **Food waste.** Finnish food services lose about **15–20% of the food they prepare**, around 16% in buffet restaurants ([Luke, Nisonen 2020](https://xamk.fi/app/uploads/sites/2/2024/12/nisonen-4.8.2020-ruokahavikki-kuriin.pdf)). Food service produces 18% of Finland's food waste, and **serving waste** (food left over on the buffet line) is the largest part ([Helsinki Uni](https://www.helsinki.fi/assets/drupal/2026-02/H%C3%A4vikin%20v%C3%A4hent%C3%A4minen%20ravitsemispalveluissa_V2.pdf)). One case measured serving waste at 19% at lunch and 13% at dinner (same source). Buffets leave roughly 300 g uneaten per guest against 130 g for à la carte **(?, vendor blog)** ([Orbisk](https://orbisk.com/blog/how-hotels-cut-buffet-food-waste-without-hurting-guest-experience/)).
- **Shortages and substitutions.** Distributors substitute a brand or drop a SKU when their fill rate falls below about 98% **(?)** ([Buyers Edge](https://buyersedgeplatform.com/?p=16358)). We found no public numbers on foodservice substitution rates. Ask Valio.
- **Recalls.** Every operator in the chain must help remove a recalled product. Ruokavirasto publishes recalls under [/elintarvikkeet/ohjeita-kuluttajille/takaisinvedot/](https://www.ruokavirasto.fi/elintarvikkeet/ohjeita-kuluttajille/takaisinvedot/) and offers **email alerts, not RSS** ([recall rules](https://www.ruokavirasto.fi/elintarvikkeet/elintarvikeala/poikkeustilanteet/takaisinveto/)). Notices name the product and the reason, and usually the batch or best-before date. Recall numbers are rising (+16% in 2021, a record) ([MTV](https://www.mtvuutiset.fi/makuja/artikkeli/ruokavirasto-ennatysmaara-elintarvikkeita-vedettiin-myynnista-tama-oli-suurin-yksittainen-syy-takaisinvetoihin-vuonna-2021/8363540)). To match a recall to orders we need GTIN plus batch, and the catalogue has GTINs.

## 4. Pain points (numbers are thin)
- **Time.** About a third of operators spend more than 3 h a week on inventory (Oracle/Technomic 2016, via [Hospitality Tech](https://hospitalitytech.com/research-reveals-how-operators-manage-labor-inventory-and-loss-prevention)). One vendor cites 8.6 h a week on ordering-related tasks for independents and 10–14 h for complex full-service menus **(?, unverified NRA citation)** ([US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/restaurant-supplier-ordering-automation-roi)). In a Choco survey, ordering and inventory rank as the most time-consuming tasks after cooking and cleaning, and one chef cut ordering from 30 to 5 minutes ([Choco 2021](https://choco.com/us/stories/time-management-restaurants-work-survey)).
- **What chefs hate.** Ordering late at night across several channels (phone, voicemail, WhatsApp, fax), re-typing the same order, short deliveries discovered only on arrival, unannounced substitutions, and unclear pack sizes ([Choco/TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1904554), [Rekki](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/rekki/id1216805389)). This is mostly vendor framing **(?)**, so it is worth asking a chef at the event.

## 5. Existing tools and the gap
| Tool | What it does | Missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Choco, Rekki** | One app to chat with and order from all suppliers. Free for restaurants. Choco converts orders into each supplier's format ([choco.com](https://choco.com/restaurants)) | Nothing about why or how much: no menu or covers reasoning |
| **MarketMan, Apicbase, Galley, meez** | Recipes and costing, inventory, suggested orders from sales and stock, allergens ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/operations-management-software/a/marketman-restaurant-inventory/compare/apicbase-food-management)) | Heavy setup (every recipe and yield typed in), no handling of anomalies |
| **FutureLog** (hotels) | Procure-to-pay: requisitions, approvals, order lists, receiving, invoices ([FutureLog](https://futurelog.com/en/our-solutions/ordering)) | Built for compliance, not for cooking decisions |
| **JAMIX** (Finnish) | Menu calendar ("Panorama") → recipes → purchase orders. Strong in the public sector and schools ([JAMIX](https://www.jamix.com/?p=28919)) | Rules-based, with no substitute reasoning or chat |
| **Wholesaler webshops** (Valio Aimo, Kespro, Meira Nova, Metro) | Templates, history, availability, delivery tracking ([Kespro Toimitukset](https://www.kespro.com/sv/ideer-och-inspiration/artiklar/kespron-uusi-toimitukset-palvelu-sujuvoittaa-arkea)) | Kitchens still build every order themselves |

**The gap:** no tool turns the menu plus covers plus events into an order, *and* reacts to the week's surprises with substitutes ranked by this kitchen's priorities, *and* explains every choice. All the pieces exist separately (recipes in JAMIX or Apicbase, carbon in My Aimo, templates in the webshop). The agent joins them.

## 6. Finnish specifics
- **Lounas.** 65% of Finns who eat lunch on workdays get it from a restaurant, and 70% prefer a buffet lunch ([Verkkouutiset](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/nama-ovat-suomalaisten-suosikkilounaat/), [Edenred](https://edenred.fi/en/blog/are-lunch-habits-in-line-with-health-goals)). Lunch costs about €13 (2023) and is partly paid by the employer lunch benefit ([Yle](https://yle.fi/t/18-216591/fi)). A lunch restaurant runs a weekly rotating menu, high volume, and a buffet with serving waste.
- **Public kitchens.** They serve about 400 million or more meals a year and buy food through tenders. Finnish origin and the share of domestic ingredients are explicit tender criteria. About 3,500 of the roughly 12,000 Hyvää Suomesta (Joutsenlippu) products are made for professional kitchens ([Ruokatieto](https://ruokatieto.fi/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/suomalainen_ruoka_julkisissa_hankinnoissa_marraskuu_2021.pdf)).
- **Origin is regulated.** Serving points must tell diners the country of origin of meat (Decree 154/2019) ([Ruokavirasto](https://ruokavirasto.fi/en/foodstuffs/food-sector/food-information/labelling/labelling-of-the-country-of-origin-of-foods/country-of-origin-of-meat-at-serving-points)). That makes "Finnish origin" a constraint for some kitchens, not just a preference.
- **Language.** Product names in the catalogue are Finnish and abbreviated ("KINKKU SAVU KYPS 2KG"). Chefs say *joulukinkku, graavilohi, silli, rosolli, lanttulaatikko*. The agent must match Finnish dish words to SKUs (see [menu-spike](menu-spike.md)).

## 7. Quantity heuristics: "120 guests, joulupöytä" (rough)
The sources disagree. Buffet protein runs 150–250 g per guest for a main ([kitchennmbrs](https://kitchennmbrs.app/en/knowledge-base/portioning-standardization/how-do-i-calculate-the-ideal-quantity-per-product-for-a-buffet-of-50-people)), vegetables 100–150 g, starch 80–200 g, and buffets need 10–15% extra over plated service ([WebstaurantStore](https://argo.webstaurantstore.com/article/1013/catering-portion-size-guide.html), [CaterBoss](https://www.caterboss.ie/article/how-do-you-calculate-catering-portions-per-person-a-comprehensive-guide)). A Finnish consumer calculation for rosolli comes to about 100 g of raw vegetables per person (Kuluttajaneuvonta, via search). Home cooks plan 400–600 g of **raw** ham per person, but that is for a home table with few other dishes ([vauva.fi](https://www.vauva.fi/keskustelu/4870594/mita-teilla-syodaan-jouluna-ja-minka-kokoinen-kinkku)).

**Our working table, per guest at a full joulupöytä (team estimate, (?), sanity-check with a chef):** ham 120–150 g cooked (about 160–200 g raw on the bone); cold fish (graavilohi, smoked salmon) 60–80 g; herring, 2–3 kinds, 40–60 g in total; three casseroles 100–150 g in total; rosolli 80–100 g; boiled potatoes 100–150 g; Karelian stew or a warm meat 80–100 g; desserts (rice porridge, prune tarts, gingerbread) about 100 g. The total is about 700–900 g of food, in line with the 600–800 g dinner-buffet totals from catering guides. **For 120 guests:** about 20–24 kg raw ham, 8–10 kg salmon, 15–18 kg casseroles, 12 kg rosolli, 15 kg potatoes. Cook to the guarantee, not the overset.

## Means for us
1. **Events are a first-class object, not a big regular order.** `Event {date, guests_estimate → guarantee (T−72h) → actual, menu, service_style: buffet|plated, overset_pct=3}`. Its lines are a separate layer that merges into the delivery-day order, so the chef can see "base + Christmas dinner".
2. **The data model needs par levels and yields.** `Recipe {dish, portions, lines: [{sku, qty_g, yield_pct}]}`, `ParLevel {sku, par, safety_pct}`, `DeliverySchedule {weekdays, cut_off}` (from availability). The regular order is `max(par − on_hand, forecast use)` rounded up to the **pack size**. Show the rounding.
3. **Cut-off is the clock.** Every proposal shows "order by Tue 14:00 for Thu delivery", taken from the availability response. If an event's guarantee lands after the cut-off, the agent says so and proposes a two-step order: the bulk now, a top-up after the guarantee.
4. **Quantity heuristics as editable rules with provenance.** Use the table in §7 as a fixture: "g per guest, buffet, source: X / team estimate". The chef changes "ham 150 → 120 g" and the order recomputes live. That edit is the interactive moment of the demo.
5. **Christmas demo script.** "Pikkujoulu buffet, Sat 12 Dec, ~120 guests, half want Finnish origin, 8 gluten-free." The agent drafts the joulupöytä from the hotel's menu, quantifies it (§7), overlays it on the regular week, flags (a) graavilohi out of stock → Finnish rainbow trout ranked by carbon and origin, (b) ham pack size 2 kg × 11 = 22 kg, (c) cut-off Wed 12:00. Then the guarantee drops to 104 at T−72h, and the agent shrinks the top-up and shows the money and kilograms of waste avoided. Possibly a Ruokavirasto recall on a herring batch on Thursday.
6. **Show waste as a number.** Finnish buffets lose about 15–20%. The chef should see "this plan vs your usual +15% habit: −X kg, −€Y, −Z kg CO₂e". This uses data we have (carbon per product) and fits Valio's own carbon reporting in My Aimo.
7. **Finnish origin can be a hard constraint**, not only a slider weight. Meat origin must be disclosed and public tenders demand it. In the profile: `origin: prefer|require FI` per category.
8. **Recalls go through a scraper or email feed, not RSS.** Match on GTIN plus batch or best-before against our stored orders. Show "affects 2 lines in Thursday's delivery and 1 kg in stock" and propose substitutes.
9. **Don't rebuild the webshop.** Valio already has templates, multiple carts and order editing. Our UI is the *reasoning layer*: event cards, a quantity breakdown (guests × g/guest → kg → packs), the substitute trade-off and "why". The output is an order the webshop could take.
10. **Questions for the Valio stand:** typical cut-off and delivery days for a Helsinki hotel; how often lines are short or substituted; whether hotels order banquets separately from the base order; whether My Aimo recipes could be a data source for us.
