# UI and data-viz patterns for Mandate's surfaces

Research, 14 Nov 2026. Question: which proven patterns from planning, control-tower, observability and process-mining tools make the system's real workings readable in under 5 seconds?

## TL;DR

Every mature tool in this space opens on **exceptions, not on the machinery**. SAP IBP and Oracle Supply Planning lead with a *projected available balance* line per product (receipts in, demand out, alert where it crosses zero or safety stock), and control towers (FourKites, project44) lead with a ranked list of things that need a human. The machinery views (waterfalls, process maps, graphs) are always the *second* click, reached from an exception. So the fix for "cool dev panel, not meaningful" is to keep Ops/Engine and put a plain-language **question on top of each one** ("Which of my products run out before Tuesday's cutoff, and who decided?"), answered by one projected-stock line or one decision queue, and to let the waterfall/op log/Sankey come after a click on a specific row (Shneiderman: overview first, then zoom and filter, then details on demand). Sankey should be cut to ≤ ~15 nodes per column (aggregate the rest) or replaced by a layered DAG or icicle for the shared-ingredient graph. Process-mining's *variant* view (the 10 most common paths through the command pipeline) is the strongest "real internals" wow that a non-engineer can still read.

## Recommended overview per page

- **Kitchen.** A Few-style single screen: next delivery date, total vs cap, and a short list of *held* lines (each with the one reason: cap, new product, allergen) as the primary action. Chat is the input, not the overview. A line's detail shows a tiny projected-stock sparkline and "why the agent chose this" as a step list (Langfuse timeline, collapsed).
- **Ops / control tower.** Lead with the exception queue, Cases grouped by cause, ranked by money × time-to-cutoff, plus 3–5 health numbers (calls/min by door, held, rejected, p95 latency). The process-map/variant view of the command pipeline (Celonis) is the "see the machine" panel, labelled in words ("97% go straight through; 2% held by mandate; 1% conflict retried"). Waterfall and op log open from a clicked row, never by default.
- **Forecast (Engine).** One product, one question: a projected-available-balance line over 14 days (Oracle/SAP), each kitchen's order as a step down, supply arrival as a step up, cutoff as a vertical rule, a red zone where stock < 0. Contention is then a Marey-style view of kitchens' claims on that product. A horizon strip of "all products at risk" sits below as the entry point.
- **Demand flow.** Aggregate to ≤ ~15 nodes per stage (top products + "other"), colour the final stage by stock status, and keep the brushable day timeline (overview + detail). Clicking a menu swaps to a layered DAG (d3-dag/elk) for that menu only, so shared ingredients show as one node with several parents and not as duplicate ribbons.
- **Architecture.** Do not draw boxes. Show the ontology as live objects (Vertex-style): pick one real Line and "search around" to its Offer, Approval (terms hash), Ops and Trace, with the doors around the edge lighting up as SSE events arrive. The real data makes it credible.

## Means for us

1. **Every page gets a question as its title and one chart that answers it.** The machinery stays, one click down from a specific exception.
2. **Projected stock line is our "Engine".** It is the industry-standard picture of supply vs demand over time, and our simulator events map onto it exactly (orders = steps down, supply.arrived = step up, cutoff.closed = rule).
3. **Ops leads with held Approvals and Cases**, ranked, not with the swimlane. The variant view of the command pipeline, in words and percentages, is the readable "real internals" wow.
4. **Demand flow: aggregate or switch layout.** ≤ ~15 nodes per Sankey stage; shared ingredients go to a layered DAG on drill-down.
5. **Library adds:** d3-sankey + d3-dag (small) are safe; @xyflow/svelte only if the ontology/pipeline graph needs drag/zoom. elkjs and embedding-atlas need lead sign-off (rule 9).
